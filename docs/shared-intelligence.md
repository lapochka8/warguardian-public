# Shared player intelligence and community ban data

**Status: proposed service.** WarGuardian is exploring an optional subscription for shared player intelligence and community ban information. No new subscription price, data coverage, API allowance or launch date is announced.

Existing private community-list sharing is a current hosted feature. This proposal would add a broader connected intelligence service; it does not turn today's private lists into a public database or change existing subscriptions.

## Proposed value

- Source-attributed moderation and ban records shared by participating communities.
- Reasons, evidence references and status updates that help staff review a record in context.
- Account context and history where the source and permitted use support it.
- Watchlists and notifications about relevant new or changed records.
- Authorized lookups for a hosted panel or a future self-hosted installation.

The useful service is maintained information: source attribution, fresh updates, access controls and a way to correct disputed or outdated records. A large unexplained list of accused players is not equivalent to reliable moderation intelligence.

## Sharing and community control

The proposed model requires explicit participation and clear publication choices. A community's private notes, tickets and evidence should not become network-visible simply because it uses WarGuardian or subscribes to intelligence.

Subscribers would receive only the records their permissions and source-sharing rules allow. Staff would retain control of their server rules and enforcement choices. A shared record is not automatically a command to ban someone everywhere.

## Evidence and identity limits

A source ban is a decision by that source. A platform ban, an observed account association and a community allegation are different kinds of information. Similar names or shared IP addresses do not prove common ownership. Incomplete coverage must remain visible; no matching record is not proof that a player has no history.

The service would need source labels, freshness information, correction and appeal handling, withdrawal behavior and clear limits for unavailable sources before launch. It would not promise perfect cheating detection or universal cross-game identity resolution.

## What a subscription would pay for

The intended charge is for ongoing authorized access to shared intelligence and its updates. It is not a charge per ban issued, an automatic purchase of other communities' private data, or a fee to export your own records.

Packages and usage limits are being evaluated. A future self-hosted panel could subscribe without moving its local server management into the hosted service. Regular local moderation should remain useful without a shared-intelligence subscription.
